# Monetization hypothesis

## Initial hypothesis (not validated)

Test a **monthly subscription for equipment-owning companies** as the first monetization hypothesis, with a limited pilot tier and a paid tier offering catalog/booking management and operational reporting. This avoids introducing multi-party money movement and platform-fee accounting before product-market evidence exists. It is a hypothesis, not a final commercial decision.

## Alternatives

- **Commission per completed hire:** aligned with successful transactions but requires trusted transaction values, payment settlement/marketplace-provider terms, chargeback, refunds and possible connected-account onboarding.
- **SaaS subscription:** predictable but asks suppliers to pay before marketplace demand is proven.
- **Paid analytics/add-ons:** requires reliable usage and data volume first.

## Interview plan

Interview 10–15 rental-company decision makers across small fleets and larger yards. Ask how they currently receive inquiries, check availability, price delivery/deposit, track service windows, handle no-shows and measure utilization. Do not pitch features until workflow pain is mapped. Measure willingness to trial, weekly active supplier operations, inquiries→bookings conversion, repeat rentals and time saved.

## Decision gates

Continue if at least several suppliers agree to a supervised pilot and repeat workflows weekly; test willingness to pay with concrete price cards rather than hypothetical yes/no questions. Stop or reposition if the catalogue is too sparse, inventory data cannot be kept current, or suppliers will not change existing dispatch workflow. Set price only after interviews and region/competitor research.

## Cost model inputs (not estimates)

Model managed database, app compute, object storage/egress, email, backups, monitoring, support, payment provider if later used, VAT/accounting and optional LLM calls. Capture actual provider quotes before setting any unit economics; no revenue guarantee is implied.
